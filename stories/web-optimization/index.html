Story 08

How do you improve customer experiences when every small change has measurable business impact?

Championed Testing and Optimization of Web Experiences

Built one of Washington Mutual's earliest web optimization programs, using experimentation and customer behavior to improve digital experiences and business outcomes.

01

The challenge

Good design is only a starting point.

When I joined Washington Mutual, most website decisions were driven by assumptions, stakeholder opinions, or established design conventions. While thoughtful design mattered, there was little objective evidence showing which experiences actually helped customers complete important tasks or improved business performance.

The opportunity was to replace opinions with measurable customer behavior by introducing structured testing and optimization across the company's digital experiences.

The most valuable digital experiences aren't designed once. They're continuously improved through learning.

02

The approach

Let customers decide.

I helped establish one of the organization's early web optimization programs, introducing A/B testing and experimentation as regular parts of the product development process.

Working closely with design, development, analytics, and business stakeholders, we identified opportunities, built experiments, analyzed customer behavior, and applied those insights to improve digital experiences over time.

Rather than treating optimization as a one-time project, we created an ongoing culture of testing that encouraged teams to validate ideas through customer behavior instead of assumptions.

The business result

Customer behavior became a strategic decision-making tool.

03

What I learned

Data should inform creativity, not replace it.

The experience reinforced that the most valuable digital experiences aren't designed once. They're refined continuously through experimentation, customer feedback, and measurable outcomes. Those lessons continue to influence how I approach product marketing, customer experience, and AI today.
