How do you help a large organization stop sounding like many teams and start sounding like one company?

Connecting the Dots Across Integrated Marketing

01

The challenge

Customers experience one brand, even when dozens of teams create it.

As organizations grow, specialized marketing teams naturally emerge. Product marketing, digital, communications, events, sales enablement, partner marketing, and creative teams all develop their own priorities and rhythms.

The result can be strong individual work but an inconsistent customer experience. My role was to help connect those efforts into a coordinated go-to-market motion that felt intentional, aligned, and customer-centered.

Integrated marketing isn't about making every team do the same thing. It's about helping every team tell the same story.

02

The approach

Create alignment before creating assets.

I worked across product marketing, communications, digital, sales, partner, and event organizations to establish shared messaging, campaign priorities, and launch plans before execution began.

Rather than optimizing each channel independently, we focused on creating connected customer journeys so every touchpoint reinforced the same value proposition while allowing each team to contribute its unique strengths.

The business result

More consistent campaigns across marketing disciplines.

Cross-functionalMarketing alignment
SharedMessaging and campaign planning

Helped create stronger collaboration across marketing functions, enabling more cohesive launches and a more consistent experience for customers, sellers, and partners.

03

What I learned

Alignment is a competitive advantage.

Some of the most valuable marketing work never appears in a campaign. It happens beforehand, when teams build shared understanding, establish common priorities, and commit to telling one coherent story.

When organizations align around the customer instead of organizational boundaries, every marketing investment becomes more effective.
