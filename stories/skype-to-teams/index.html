Story 02

How do you retire a service used by tens of millions of customers without disrupting the work they rely on every day?

Guiding One of Microsoft's Largest Customer Transitions

Led the communications strategy for the retirement of Skype for Business Online, helping guide tens of millions of customers to Microsoft Teams through one of the company's largest service migrations.

01

The challenge

Retire an aging platform while protecting customer confidence.

Most go-to-market efforts introduce customers to something new. This was the opposite.

Microsoft needed to retire Skype for Business Online, sunsetting an increasingly expensive, redundant, and aging technology platform in favor of Microsoft Teams. At the same time, many customers viewed Teams as broader than they needed and were understandably cautious about changing a service central to daily business communication.

The challenge wasn't simply communicating that Skype was going away. We had to build confidence in the transition while ensuring customers could continue running their businesses throughout the migration.

Success wasn't measured by awareness. It was measured by customers choosing to migrate on their own timeline instead of having the decision made for them.

Successful change management isn't about convincing people to embrace change. It's about reducing uncertainty enough that they feel confident taking the next step.

02

The approach

Design the transition around the complete customer journey.

I led the communications strategy for what we often referred to as a reverse go-to-market, partnering across engineering, product, support, sales, and leadership to coordinate every stage of the customer journey.

Together, we created inbound and outbound communications, in-product messaging, tiered support models, self-service and assisted migration experiences, and readiness materials for customers, partners, and the field.

Because no migration of this scale comes with perfect information, we projected customer behaviors, migration velocity, support demand, and extension requests. We then continuously refined our approach as real-world data and customer feedback became available.

The work required consistent alignment across organizations with different responsibilities and time horizons, while keeping customer confidence and business continuity at the center of every decision.

The business result

Customers moved to Teams while keeping their businesses running.

10M+ Customers represented across the transition
Global Customer, partner, seller, and support readiness

The program helped guide tens of millions of Skype for Business Online customers to Microsoft Teams while supporting business continuity throughout one of Microsoft's largest service transitions.

03

What I learned

Confidence is the real currency of change.

Large-scale transitions rarely succeed because every customer is enthusiastic about the destination. They succeed when people understand what is changing, why it matters, what is expected of them, and where to turn for help.

That requires more than a launch announcement. It requires a coordinated experience that anticipates uncertainty, gives customers meaningful choices, and provides reinforcement throughout the journey.

The Skype for Business Online retirement reinforced a lesson that has shaped my work ever since: effective change management reduces uncertainty one practical step at a time.
