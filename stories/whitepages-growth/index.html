Story 01 Whitepages

How do you grow faster by improving the product before increasing the marketing?

Unlocking Growth Through Customer-Centric Product and Go-to-Market Strategy

Whitepages was growing rapidly, but acquisition costs were climbing and customer behavior suggested untapped opportunities. Rather than focusing exclusively on new campaigns, we asked a different question: What if the product itself could become the engine of growth?

Story overview

01

The challenge

Growth opportunities were hiding in customer behavior.

When I joined Whitepages, the company had tremendous momentum. Millions of people relied on the service every month, yet many valuable customer needs remained underserved.

Growth opportunities were not hidden in more advertising. They were hidden in customer behavior.

The challenge was understanding what customers were actually trying to accomplish, then translating those insights into better products, clearer positioning, and a business model that supported long-term growth.

Marketing was not simply responsible for communicating value. It became part of shaping the value itself.

02

The approach

Connect product, pricing, research, and go-to-market.

Rather than treating marketing, product, pricing, and customer research as separate disciplines, we connected them.

We studied customer behavior to uncover unmet needs. We identified friction points that prevented customers from discovering premium capabilities. We worked across product, engineering, design, and executive leadership to prioritize features that solved real customer problems while strengthening the business model.

This changed the role of product marketing. It was no longer limited to packaging and promotion after a product decision had been made. It became an active partner in deciding what should be built, how it should be positioned, and how the experience could better support customer and business goals.

The business result

A more integrated growth strategy delivered measurable impact.

350%+ Division revenue growth in 2004
75% Additional revenue growth in 2005

The strategy also improved alignment between customer needs, product investment, and go-to-market execution, creating a stronger foundation for sustainable product expansion.

03

What I learned

The best product marketing begins with curiosity.

One lesson from Whitepages has stayed with me throughout my career.

The most effective product marketing does not begin with messaging. It begins with curiosity.

When teams deeply understand customer problems, product decisions improve, positioning becomes clearer, and marketing becomes significantly more effective because it reflects genuine customer value instead of simply describing features.

That philosophy has influenced how I have approached every role since, from leading major service transitions at Microsoft to developing AI-powered competitive intelligence.
