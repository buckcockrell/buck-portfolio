Story 06

How do you help thousands of people embrace a new way of working without disrupting the customers they support?

Leading Change at Global Scale

Helped launch Microsoft's next-generation support knowledge platform, guiding thousands of support agents worldwide through a phased transition without disrupting customer support.

01

The challenge

Introducing a new platform is rarely simply a technology project.

It's an exercise in change management. When Microsoft Customer Service and Support introduced a new knowledge platform for its global support organization, thousands of support agents needed to learn new tools and workflows without disrupting the quality or speed of customer support.

The challenge was made even more complex by the need to operate both the existing and new knowledge platforms in parallel until the transition was complete.

Successful organizational change depends as much on people as it does on technology.

02

The approach

Treat adoption as a journey, not a launch.

Working across engineering, program management, business stakeholders, and agent teams, I helped establish and implement the communications, readiness, and change management strategy that guided the organization through the transition.

That included executive communications, launch planning, readiness materials, training resources, ongoing awareness campaigns, and adoption guidance that prepared employees for each phase of the rollout.

Rather than treating launch as a single event, we approached adoption as a continuous journey, providing timely information and reinforcement as employees gradually shifted from one platform to the other.

The business result

A successful global rollout without disrupting business continuity.

ThousandsSupport agents

Supported the successful rollout of a new global support knowledge platform through coordinated communications, readiness, and change management, enabling thousands of support agents to transition while maintaining business continuity.

03

What I learned

People adopt change when they have confidence, not just information.

The experience reinforced that successful organizational change depends as much on people as it does on technology. The most effective change management doesn't simply announce what's changing. It gives people the confidence to embrace new tools, resources, and ways of working while continuing to deliver for customers.
